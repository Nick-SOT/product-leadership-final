## Write your one-page strategy
- **Winning aspiration: define winning in the customer's terms, not internal metrics. What does success look like for the specific person you serve?:** Become the OT security platform industrial organisations and their security partners rely on to act on what cyber risk means for their operations, before and during a cyberattack. For the leader accountable for OT security, winning means answering the board’s “what happens if we’re hit?” in production, safety and euros, showing what’s funded to shrink that answer, and, when an attack comes, seeing their responders protect what matters most first.
- **Where to play: segment, geography, and use case, explicitly. Which customers, markets, and surfaces will you focus on?:** Customers: mid-sized and large producers with three or more industrial sites. 2027: Manufacturing

Geographic focus: EU first, with one NIS2 core and a national delta per country: Belgium, Netherlands, Luxembourg, France.

Use cases: two entry points, one destination. Enter through compliance when the trigger is an audit or a deadline, or through response when the trigger is an incident or a SOC that can’t act on OT alerts. Both build the customer’s own consequence model, which then prioritises investment before an attack and compares the operational consequences of response options during one.

Buyers, response entry: the IT MSSP, which licenses the platform to add OT to its managed service; the customer’s CISO buys that service.
Channel: direct in Belgium; resellers and distributors for the compliance entry; IT MSSPs as service partners for the response entry, with Soterics as their OT expertise layer.

- **How to win: your differentiator. What can you do that your specific competitors cannot easily replicate?:** Sensor vendors score risk from network data; consultancies capture consequences once and leave. We keep each customer’s own consequence model live, explainable and sensor-agnostic, captured from the people who run the plant, so every decision before and during an attack shows its working. ## Name your one hard no
- **Your one hard no:** We will not manage enterprise IT (Purdue L4–L5): no IT inventory, vulnerability management, monitoring or compliance scope. IT enters our platform only where it can cause an OT consequence, as an attack path or a dependency. This keeps our depth in OT and keeps us complementary to the IT MSSPs that resell us.

Positioning line: “We model IT. We don’t manage it.”
Sharper variant: “IT is context, never scope.”

## Write your three OKRs
- **Objective:** Prove with three multi-site producers that customers act on their own consequences, before and during a cyberattack.
- **KR1:** Roadmaps approved with committed CAPEX/OPEX, with priority work packs traced to a business threat scenario: 0 → 10 across 3 customers.
- **KR2:** Median time for the customer’s CSIRT or partner SOC to reach a response decision (contain, isolate, switch to manual) that protects the top-ranked consequence, in OT incident exercises: baseline (same format, without the consequence view) → −50% at the same 3 customers.
- **KR3:** Share of risks above the customer’s risk appetite with a customer-named owner and a recorded treatment decision: 0 → 50% at each of 3 customers.
